Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 28, 2026

Moss is an iPhone app to log what you eat, your weight, your body measurements, your sleep and your habits. This is what data it uses, where it stays and what you can do with it.

  • Moss doesn’t sell your data, doesn’t show ads and doesn’t track you.
  • Almost everything happens on your iPhone. With your account, what you log is also saved on our server, to sync it and keep it safe.
  • What Moss reads from Apple Health is only used for the app. Never for advertising.
  • If you link Moss with Ascent, our gym app, the two share the data listed below. Only if you choose to, and you can undo it at any time.
  • From Profile you can export your data and delete your data or your account.
  • Moss is for people 18 and older.

Who is responsible

Agustín Conde Zuzulich (“we”), located in Montevideo, Uruguay, is the controller of Moss data. For any privacy matter, email us at nodoexpweb@gmail.com.

What data Moss uses

  • Your account. You sign in with Apple (“Sign in with Apple”). Apple gives us an account ID and your email address or, if you choose to hide it, an Apple relay address; the first time, also your name, if you share it. Moss has no passwords of its own. Why: to sign in and sync. Where: the server.
  • Your profile. Your name: the one you shared when signing in with Apple or the one you write in Profile (it can be a nickname, and it’s only shown in your Profile). Your year of birth, sex, height, how active you are, your goal and its pace, how you split your macros, your goal weight and the day of your weekly check-in. If you build a meal plan, how you eat: your type of diet, your allergies, how many meals you have, how much time you have to cook and what you like and don’t like. If you tell us you’re pregnant, breastfeeding or have a medical condition, Moss only saves that your targets have no deficit, not the reason. Why: to calculate and adjust your targets and build your plan. Where: your phone and your account. Your profile photo, on the other hand, stays only on your phone.
  • What you eat. Each entry (the food, the amount, the meal, the day and time, the calories and nutrients), what you planned, your recipes and saved meals, your own foods (with their barcode), your favorites and your water. Where: your phone and your account.
  • Your body and your targets. Your daily weight, your measurements (waist, neck, hips, arm, chest and thigh) and your calorie and macro targets, with the estimated expenditure behind them. The body fat estimate is calculated on your phone. Where: your phone and your account.
  • Your habits. Their name, icon and days, whether you complete them by hand or automatically (with Apple Health or, if you link Ascent, with your workouts) and with what goal (for example, 8,000 steps), what you checked off each day, each day’s summary and your achievements. Where: your phone and your account.
  • Your sleep, what Moss knows. How you said you slept and, if you don’t wear a watch, the time Moss estimates you fell asleep and woke up, with what you confirmed or corrected. Where: your phone and your account.
  • Only on your phone. Your meal photos and your profile photo; what Open Food Facts returned for the products you scanned; the meal you’re putting together and your shopping list; your reminders and settings (including Profile › Training); your alarm and the times your Shortcuts record; what the widgets show; and when you opened Moss in the last three days (used to estimate your night). Photos are included in your iPhone backup if you have it turned on; Moss never uploads them. Where: only your phone.

On your iPhone, Moss stores your data encrypted. We use your data only to make Moss work. We don’t use it for advertising or to build profiles.

Apple Health

If you allow it, Moss reads from Apple Health: date of birth, sex, height, weight, steps, water, mindful minutes, sleep, heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability and respiratory rate. Moss never writes to Apple Health.

Of all that, only this reaches your account:

  • your year of birth, sex and height, if you fill in the questionnaire with Apple Health;
  • your weight, on the days you didn’t log it by hand;
  • whether the habits that turn on with Apple Health were completed (yes or no, without the amount);
  • the estimated time of your night without a watch, which comes from your steps and your phone’s motion.

Everything else (steps, your watch’s nights, heart rate, variability, respiration) is read and used only on your phone. Apple Health data is never used for advertising or marketing and never sold. It’s only shared with Ascent if you link the two apps (see “Ascent”), and with no one else. You can change the permission at any time in your iPhone’s Settings: Privacy & Security › Health › Moss.

Motion, camera, photos and microphone

Motion. If you don’t wear a watch and you allow it, Moss reads your iPhone’s activity history (still, walking, driving) to estimate your night. That history never leaves your phone: only the estimated time is saved.

Camera. To scan barcodes and nutrition labels, to take a meal photo and to take your profile photo. Labels are read on your phone, with the iPhone’s own text recognition.

Photos. To choose your profile photo, Moss uses the iPhone’s photo picker: it only receives the photo you pick and doesn’t ask for access to your library.

Microphone and speech recognition. With “Say what I ate”, Moss listens only while you speak and turns what you say into text with Apple’s speech recognition: on your phone when your language allows it and, if not, on Apple’s servers, which handle it under Apple’s own policy. Moss never saves the audio. The text is only used to find the foods: what stays are the entries you confirm.

The alarm and Shortcuts

The alarm. On iOS 26 or later, if you set the alarm in Moss, Moss asks your iPhone to ring at the time and on the days you choose, and records the time you stop it, to know when you woke up. You can allow it or not, and change it in your iPhone’s Settings.

Shortcuts. Moss offers actions in the Shortcuts app for the automations you build: when you stop an alarm, when you connect or disconnect the charger, or when the Sleep Focus starts or ends. When one runs, Moss only records which one it was and at what time: it doesn’t know which alarm it was or anything about your battery.

The times from the alarm and from Shortcuts stay on your phone. Only the estimated night that comes from them reaches your account, as always.

Widgets

Moss widgets show today’s habits in your sky (on the Home Screen) and the night Moss estimated, so you can confirm it with a tap (on the Lock Screen). To do that, Moss keeps a copy of what they show in a space on your iPhone that it only shares with its widgets, and what you tap on a widget stays there until you open Moss. To keep them up to date, your iPhone lets Moss refresh in the background from time to time, doing the same as when you open it.

What a widget shows on the Lock Screen can be seen without unlocking your iPhone. If you don’t want that, don’t add it, or remove it.

Ascent

Ascent is another app of ours, for the gym. You can link it with Moss if you want: Moss works just the same without it.

What each app shares

  • Moss shares with Ascent: how long you slept and how you said you slept, what you eat summarized by day (calories and protein), your weight and your goal (lose, maintain or gain).
  • Ascent shares with Moss: your workouts (the day, when you started, the minutes, the exercises with their muscle group and the sets with their weight and reps), your personal records, the weight you log in Ascent and your Ascent username.

With that, Moss shows you your recovery, the performance you can expect, good days to try a personal record, one weight for both apps, calories by day, protein after training and the gym habit, which lights up on its own. Ascent uses Moss’s data for the same on its side. Nothing is copied from one account to the other: each app reads the other’s data while they’re linked.

Only with your permission

Linking is a step you take. Before it, Moss shows you what each app shares, and nothing is shared until you tap “Link”. Because it includes health data, it’s explicit consent, which you withdraw by unlinking.

Two accounts

Your Moss account and your Ascent account are two accounts, each with its own information, even if you use the same email or the same Apple Account in both. Linking connects them so they share this data; it doesn’t merge them into one. You can link them even if they use different emails or Apple Accounts.

How they link

  • With both apps on your iPhone: when you sign in, each app leaves its session in the iPhone’s keychain (a token that expires in one hour) and Ascent also leaves your username. That part of the keychain can only be read by apps from our same Apple development team. When you tap “Link”, Moss sends both sessions to our server, which checks them and saves the link between your two accounts. The token isn’t stored on the server, and each app deletes its own from the keychain when you sign out.
  • Signing in to your Ascent account inside Moss: you enter the email address and password of your Ascent account. Moss uses them once, to get that session and link, and doesn’t keep them.

Unlinking

In Moss, in Profile › Connections › Ascent; in Ascent, in its Settings. Unlinking removes the link in both apps: each one stops reading the other’s data, and each keeps its own. If you delete your Moss account or your Ascent account, the link is deleted with it, and the other one stays as it is.

Open Food Facts

When you scan a code Moss doesn’t know, your phone asks Open Food Facts, an open food database, about that code. It receives only the code and, like any website, the IP address of the connection. It doesn’t receive your account or anything else.

App updates

Besides App Store versions, Moss can download improvements when it opens, through Expo’s update service. To do that, your phone asks Expo whether there’s a new version: it sends the app version, the iOS version and a random identifier for this installation, which isn’t tied to your account or to anything about you, and which Expo uses to deliver updates and count how many installations receive them. Expo doesn’t receive your Moss data.

To improve the food database

  • Searches with no results. If you allow it, Moss sends what you searched for when it found nothing: the text, the app’s language and region, and the day. You can turn this off in Profile › Privacy and data.
  • Report a problem. If you tap “Report a problem” on a food, Moss sends which food it is, what’s wrong, your note if you write one, and the day.

Both are stored without your account or any identifier, and are only used to fix and complete the database. Because they aren’t tied to you, we can’t find or delete them later: don’t write personal information in the note.

Who else handles your data

  • Supabase, Inc. hosts the database, the accounts and the server functions (such as linking Ascent and deleting your account), as a processor: it handles the data only to provide that service to us, under a data processing agreement. Its servers are outside Uruguay, on Amazon Web Services.
  • Apple, when you sign in with Apple (it confirms who you are and gives us an ID and an email address) and when it recognizes your voice off your phone, as explained above. Apple Health stays on your iPhone: Apple doesn’t receive from Moss what’s in it.
  • Expo (650 Industries, Inc.) delivers the app updates, as a processor: it only receives what’s explained in “App updates” and, like any website, the IP address. Its servers are outside Uruguay.
  • Open Food Facts, only the barcode, as explained above.

No one else. Moss doesn’t use analytics, advertising or other third-party services that collect data, and doesn’t share your data with artificial intelligence services. Anyone who handles data for us commits to protecting it as well as or better than this policy says. We would only hand over data if a law or a valid court order requires it.

Ascent isn’t a third party: it’s ours, and it protects what it receives from Moss as this policy says. Even so, it only receives Moss data if you link the two apps.

Your consent

  • The account and syncing are the service you ask for when you create your account.
  • We handle your health data (what you eat, your weight, your measurements, your sleep, your allergies and what you read from Apple Health) with your explicit consent, which you give in the app. You can withdraw it at any time: by deleting your data or your account, or by removing permissions in your iPhone’s Settings.
  • Sharing data between Moss and Ascent is a separate explicit consent: you give it by tapping “Link” and withdraw it by unlinking.
  • Your data is stored outside Uruguay. By creating your account you accept that transfer, as Uruguayan Law 18,331 (article 23) allows.

How long we keep it

  • In your account: as long as you have it. If you delete an entry, it stays marked as deleted so it’s also deleted on your other phones; it’s gone for good when you delete your data or your account.
  • The link with Ascent: until you undo it. It’s deleted when you unlink or delete your account.
  • The session an app leaves in the keychain: it expires in one hour, is replaced every time you sign in and is deleted when you sign out.
  • Server backups: deleted data may remain in them for up to 7 days. They replace themselves and aren’t used for anything else.
  • Server technical logs (such as the IP address of each connection): Supabase keeps them for a few days, for security.
  • On your phone: until you sign out, delete your data or uninstall Moss.
  • Searches with no results and reports: up to 12 months.

Export, correct, unlink and delete

  • Take your data with you: Profile › Privacy and data › Export everything gives you what you ate, your weight and your targets as CSV. If you want a copy of everything else, email us.
  • Correct: everything can be edited in the app.
  • Unlink Ascent: Profile › Connections › Ascent, or in Ascent’s Settings.
  • Delete your Moss data (the account stays): Profile › Privacy and data › Delete my data.
  • Delete your account: Profile › Privacy and data › Delete account. Your Moss account and everything in Moss are deleted right away, on the server and on your phone, and Moss’s access to your Apple Account is revoked as well. If it’s linked with Ascent, the link is deleted; your Ascent account stays as it is and just stops getting your Moss data. If you use the same email in both, only your way into Moss is removed: nothing in Ascent is touched.
  • Permissions: Apple Health, camera, microphone, speech recognition, motion, notifications and alarms are removed in your iPhone’s Settings; searches with no results, in Profile; widgets, from your screen; and automations, in the Shortcuts app.

In Uruguay, Law 18,331 gives you the right to access your data and to ask for it to be corrected, updated, added to or deleted. We answer within 5 business days. If you’re not satisfied, you can complain to the Unidad Reguladora y de Control de Datos Personales (URCDP). If you live somewhere else with privacy laws, such as the European Union or the United Kingdom, you also have the rights they give you (for example, portability or objection) and you can complain to your authority.

Security

  • All connections are encrypted (HTTPS).
  • On your iPhone, your data is stored encrypted.
  • On the server, each row belongs to one account, and the database rules don’t let anyone see another account’s rows. Ascent’s data is only read through a link you made, and linking needs the live sessions of both accounts.
  • If a security problem affected your data, we would tell you and the authority, as the law requires.

Minors

Moss is for people 18 and older, and we don’t knowingly collect data from minors. If you think a minor created an account, email us and we’ll delete it.

Changes

If we change something important in this policy, we’ll tell you in the app before it takes effect. The date of the latest version is at the top.
